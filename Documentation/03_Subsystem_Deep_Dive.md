# Subsystem Deep Dive: Engineering Architecture and Algorithms

This document provides a comprehensive engineering-depth analysis of the core subsystems that comprise the ATF V-2.1 Edge-Processed Structural Health Monitoring (SHM) Node. Each section details the physical principles, hardware specifications, signal processing methodology, algorithmic parameters, and concrete highway bridge-specific application notes.

Phase 2 stretch enhancements are covered briefly at the end of this document.

---

## Subsystem 1: Acoustic Emission (Micro-Fracture Detection)

### Purpose and Physical Principle
The primary purpose of the Acoustic Emission (AE) subsystem is to detect the high-frequency elastic waves generated by micro-fractures, crack propagation, aggregate-matrix debonding, or rebar slip within the concrete structure. A piezoelectric contact microphone is utilized; the piezoelectric crystal generates a proportional voltage when subjected to mechanical stress (sound waves traveling through the solid medium).

### Hardware Specification
*   **Sensor:** High-frequency piezoelectric contact microphone.
*   **Analog Front-End:** LM358 operational amplifier acting as a comparator.
*   **Interrupt Circuit:** The comparator circuit features adjustable hysteresis to provide a clean, debounced digital trigger when the analog signal crosses a predefined hardware threshold.
*   **Microcontroller Interface:** Routed to a hardware interrupt pin on the ESP32-S3 (Core 1).

### Signal Processing Method
The system employs an event-triggered architecture. The node remains in deep sleep until the comparator triggers the hardware interrupt. Once awake, Core 1 executes a Daubechies wavelet transform on the captured audio burst. 
*   **Why Wavelets?** Unlike Fast Fourier Transforms (FFT), which assume periodic signals and lose time localization, wavelets offer excellent time-frequency resolution. The Daubechies wavelet is chosen due to its compact support, which closely matches the transient, non-periodic shape of fracture snaps.
*   **Decomposition Levels:** The signal is decomposed into approximation (low frequency) and detail (high frequency) coefficients. Detail coefficients at levels 1-3 correspond to the high-frequency signature of snaps.
*   **Energy Thresholding:** The energy (sum of squared detail coefficients) is calculated. If the energy exceeds a tuned threshold, it confirms a potential micro-fracture event and provides a precise timestamp for downstream processing. Approximation coefficients are largely rejected to filter out low-frequency noise (e.g., traffic rumble, wind).

### Algorithm Parameters and Tuning
*   **Sampling Rate:** Minimum 44.1 kHz during the interrupt window.
*   **Wavelet Type:** `db4` or `db8` (tuning dependent on exact concrete response).
*   **Energy Threshold:** Field-tuned to separate aggregate popping from traffic impacts.
*   **Window Size:** 10-20 ms capture buffer post-interrupt.

### Input and Output Format
*   **Input:** Analog voltage transient from piezo.
*   **Output:** Precise timestamp of the snap, wavelet energy score, and boolean flag triggering the strain correlation window.

### Edge Cases and Failure Modes
*   **False Triggers:** Heavy traffic impacts (e.g., a truck hitting a pothole) might trigger the comparator. The wavelet energy filter is designed to reject these based on frequency characteristics.
*   **Failure Mode:** Piezoelectric crystal degradation over years or debonding from the concrete surface, leading to loss of sensitivity (monitored by Subsystem 5).

### Relevance to Concrete Highway Bridges
In reinforced concrete, acoustic emissions typically arise from aggregate-matrix debonding (initial micro-cracking), crack propagation through the concrete paste, or rebar slip. These events have distinct high-frequency signatures compared to elastic traffic load.

## Subsystem 2: Strain and Deformation

### Purpose and Physical Principle
Measures the physical bending, elongation, or compression of the structure. It utilizes foil strain gauges where deformation of the metallic foil changes its electrical resistance proportional to the applied strain (defined by the Gauge Factor).

### Hardware Specification
* **Sensor:** Foil strain gauge (e.g., BF350).
* **Circuit:** Quarter-bridge Wheatstone configuration with precision balancing resistors. The output voltage relates to resistance change via: $V_{out} = V_{ex} \times \left( \frac{\Delta R}{4R} \right)$.
* **Amplifier/ADC:** HX711 (or ADS1115).
  * **Resolution:** 24-bit.
  * **Gain:** 128x for Channel A to detect microvolt changes.
  * **Sampling Rate:** Tunable (typically 10 SPS or 80 SPS for burst).

### Signal Processing Method
When Subsystem 1 flags a snap, it establishes a precise timestamp and opens a monitoring window for Subsystem 2. A Page-Hinkley (PH) changepoint detection test is applied to the thermally-compensated strain data.
*   **Page-Hinkley Algorithm:** Cumulatively monitors the sum of deviations from the current mean. It detects shifts in the mean level of the signal.
    *   $m_t = \sum_{i=1}^t (x_i - \bar{x} - \delta)$
    *   $M_t = \max(m_1, m_2, ..., m_t)$
    *   Shift occurs if $M_t - m_t > \lambda$
*   **Causal Correlation:** If the PH test confirms a permanent baseline shift (non-elastic deformation) within the post-acoustic-event window, it is classified as a "causally confirmed fracture candidate." If the strain returns to baseline (elastic) or drifts without an acoustic transient, it is rejected.

### Algorithm Parameters and Tuning
*   **$\lambda$ (Detection Threshold):** Sets the sensitivity to permanent deformation.
*   **$\delta$ (Allowance/Tolerance):** Minimum shift magnitude to accumulate.
*   **$\alpha$ (Minimum Amplitude):** Baseline minimum change to prevent noise accumulation.

### Input and Output Format
*   **Input:** Thermally-compensated strain time-series array.
*   **Output:** Boolean shift detected flag, shift magnitude, and correlation confidence score.

### Edge Cases and Failure Modes
*   **Edge Case:** Slow plastic deformation over years (creep) without acoustic snaps. Subsystem 3 and long-term strain polling catch this, distinct from fracture events.
*   **Failure Mode:** Lead wire resistance changes due to temperature (mostly handled by proper bridge wiring) or gauge debonding.

### Relevance to Concrete Highway Bridges
Typical elastic flex under heavy traffic loads ranges from 50-200 microstrain. Permanent shifts >500 microstrain often indicate localized yield or structural concern requiring human inspection.

## Subsystem 3: Spatial Tilt and Settling

### Purpose and Physical Principle
Monitors the long-term spatial orientation of the node to detect foundation settling, pier tilt, or gross structural rotation using MEMS accelerometers and gyroscopes.

### Hardware Specification
*   **Sensor:** 6-axis MEMS IMU. BNO085 is preferred over MPU6050 due to its onboard sensor fusion processor (providing pre-calculated quaternions) and lower long-term bias drift.
*   **Interface:** I2C to ESP32-S3 (polled by Core 0).

### Signal Processing Method
*   **Tilt Calculation:** Pitch and roll are calculated from the gravity vector measured by the accelerometer using $\text{atan2}(a_y, a_z)$ and $\text{atan2}(-a_x, \sqrt{a_y^2 + a_z^2})$.
*   **Drift Discrimination:** Long-term tilt trends are compared against a historical noise floor envelope. If the rate of change exceeds the known envelope, it indicates either physical settling or sensor failure.

### Algorithm Parameters and Tuning
*   **Moving Average Window:** Large (e.g., 24-hour average) to filter transient vibrations from traffic.

### Input and Output Format
*   **Input:** Triaxial acceleration data.
*   **Output:** Absolute pitch and roll angles (degrees), long-term drift rate.

### Edge Cases and Failure Modes
*   **Failure Mode:** Inherent MEMS bias drift over years. Differentiating slow IMU drift from actual structural settling is complex and relies on Subsystem 5's multivariate analysis.

### Relevance to Concrete Highway Bridges
Concrete bridge piers and abutments can suffer from scour or foundation settling. While typical settling rates are very slow (millimeters per year), angular tilt exceeding established thresholds (e.g., >0.1 degrees sustained shift) flags serious foundation concerns.

## Subsystem 4: Environmental Compensation

### Purpose and Physical Principle
Concrete and steel expand and contract with temperature changes, heavily influencing raw strain measurements. This subsystem builds a structure-specific thermal model to isolate load-induced strain from thermally-induced strain.

### Hardware Specification
*   **Sensor:** BME280 Environmental Sensor.
    *   **Accuracy:** Temperature ±1°C, Humidity ±3%.
*   **Interface:** I2C.

### Signal Processing Method
*   **Calibration Phase:** During the first 14 days, the node operates autonomously, logging paired (temperature, raw strain) samples over full diurnal cycles to capture maximum gradients.
*   **Regression Modeling:** Fits a regression model (typically linear for concrete, though polynomial can be used if non-linear bounds are observed) to establish the thermal-expansion coefficient.
    *   Validation uses R² to ensure the model accurately captures thermal effects.
*   **Compensation:** During runtime, $\text{Strain}_{compensated} = \text{Strain}_{raw} - f(\text{Temperature})$.

### Algorithm Parameters and Tuning
*   **Sampling Strategy:** Polled every 15-30 minutes during calibration to conserve power while capturing diurnal curves.
*   **Recalibration Trigger:** Seasonal shifts (e.g., winter to summer) trigger a periodic 3-day recalibration to adjust for seasonal baseline changes.

### Input and Output Format
*   **Input:** Raw temperature and raw strain.
*   **Output:** Compensated strain value.

### Edge Cases and Failure Modes
*   **Edge Case:** Rapid, uneven solar heating (one side of the bridge in sun, the other in shade) can cause complex thermal warping not perfectly captured by a single localized temperature reading.
*   **Failure Mode:** BME280 failure leaves strain uncompensated, resulting in false daily changepoints.

### Relevance to Concrete Highway Bridges
The typical thermal expansion coefficient for concrete is ~10-12 × 10⁻⁶ /°C. However, this varies significantly by specific mix design, age, moisture content, and reinforcement density. A self-calibrating model is crucial as a hardcoded coefficient is frequently inaccurate.

## Subsystem 5: Sensor-Fault Discrimination (Mahalanobis Distance)

### Purpose and Physical Principle
Differentiates between actual structural damage and sensor failures (e.g., debonding gauges, degraded piezo, drifting IMU). A single sensor behaving anomalously while others remain normal suggests a sensor fault.

### Signal Processing Method
*   **Multivariate Approach:** Treats the full reading vector $X = [\text{acoustic\_energy}, \text{strain\_reading}, \text{tilt\_angle}, \text{temperature}]$ as a single point in multivariate space.
*   **Covariance Matrix Construction:** During calibration and normal operation, the system maintains a covariance matrix $S$ representing the "normal joint sensor behavior" (e.g., how strain naturally correlates with temperature).
*   **Mahalanobis Distance ($D_M$):** Measures the distance of a new reading vector $x$ from the mean distribution $\mu$, factoring in the covariance.
    *   $D_M(x) = \sqrt{(x - \mu)^T S^{-1} (x - \mu)}$
*   **Evaluation:** If a specific channel causes a high $D_M$ while inter-channel correlations break down (e.g., strain jumps drastically but temperature and acoustic remain entirely flat), the system flags a sensor fault rather than a structural alarm.

### Algorithm Parameters and Tuning
*   **Thresholding:** Based on the Chi-squared distribution. The degrees of freedom equal the number of sensors. A 99% confidence interval defines the boundary of "normal" behavior.

### Input and Output Format
*   **Input:** Vector of current sensor readings.
*   **Output:** Multivariate anomaly score, and specific sensor fault flag if identified.

### Fault Catalog in Mahalanobis Space
*   **Debonding Strain Gauge:** Strain drops sharply to zero/baseline independently of temperature; high $D_M$.
*   **Degraded Piezo:** Acoustic energy consistently zero despite traffic strain transients.

### Relevance to Concrete Highway Bridges
Harsh highway environments (salt, vibration, extreme weather) accelerate sensor degradation. Independent thresholds often fail here; Mahalanobis distance effectively prevents costly manual bridge inspections triggered by a simple sensor failure.

## Subsystem 6: Multi-Node Cross-Validation

### Purpose and Physical Principle
Provides distributed consensus to validate anomalies without relying on cloud infrastructure. Prevents localized noise (e.g., a bird strike on the enclosure) from triggering a bridge-wide alert.

### Hardware Specification
*   **Communication:** SX1278 LoRa Transceiver operating in the 865-867 MHz India ISM band. Mesh networking topology.

### Signal Processing Method
*   **Provisional Alert Protocol:** When a node detects an anomaly, it broadcasts an event descriptor containing: `[Node ID, Precise Timestamp, Confidence Score, Sensor Summary]`.
*   **Time-Window Agreement:** Neighboring nodes receive the broadcast and check their local buffers. They must independently find a correlated anomaly (e.g., strain wave arrival) within a narrow time-offset tolerance (calculated via wave propagation speed in concrete).
*   **Consensus Logic:** 
    *   If multiple nodes agree within the time/confidence window $\rightarrow$ Final structural alert transmitted.
    *   If only the originating node detects it $\rightarrow$ Provisional/suppressed (likely a localized impact or single-node fault).

### Algorithm Parameters and Tuning
*   **Time Offset Tolerance:** Based on the speed of sound in concrete (~3000-4000 m/s) and node spacing.
*   **Confidence Threshold:** Minimum combined score required for consensus.

### Input and Output Format
*   **Input:** Local provisional alerts and LoRa broadcast packets.
*   **Output:** Confirmed mesh-level alert or localized suppression log.

### Relevance to Concrete Highway Bridges
Highway bridges span large distances. A genuine structural failure (like a tendon snap or major girder crack) will propagate energy across multiple nodes. False positives like animal activity or localized debris impacts will not satisfy the spatial validation criteria.

## Phase 2 Stretch Enhancements (Future)

> [!NOTE]
> The following components are **NOT** part of the current prototype scope and are documented here strictly as future architectural enhancements.

### 7. Physics-Informed Residual Modeling ("Digital Twin")
*   **Concept:** A lightweight, localized beam-deflection model runs on the edge node. By estimating the passing load (using strain profiles), the model predicts the *expected* strain.
*   **Method:** The system calculates the residual difference between the predicted structural response and the observed response. A growing residual over time indicates structural stiffness degradation.

### 8. Dempster-Shafer Evidence Fusion
*   **Concept:** Replaces simpler boolean/threshold logic with a formal mathematical framework for reasoning with uncertainty.
*   **Method:** Assigns "belief" and "plausibility" mass to different hypotheses (e.g., H1: Traffic, H2: Crack, H3: Sensor Fault). It combines evidence from all subsystems using Dempster's Rule of Combination, explicitly handling conflicting evidence (e.g., acoustic says fracture, but strain says no shift) to arrive at a highly robust probabilistic conclusion.
